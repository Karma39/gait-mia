\section{Membership Inference}
\label{sec:mia}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The per-subject membership signal is the delta
\begin{equation}
  \delta(s) = \bar{P}_{\text{imp}}(s) - \bar{P}_{\text{gen}}(s),
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{P}_{\text{imp}}$ and $\bar{P}_{\text{gen}}$ are mean
$P(\text{different person})$ over impostor and genuine pairs for subject~$s$.
Fig.~\ref{fig:deltas} shows the per-subject distributions; a larger horizontal
separation indicates a stronger signal.

\subsection{LiRA threat model comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:lira-threat} compares raw-delta AUC across three threat models.
Grey-box initialises shadows from the actual target weights; warm uses an
independently trained surrogate; cold starts from random Xavier init. All use
member-aware splits ($K{=}4$ quick-mode shadows).
Cold-start consistently outperforms warm across all datasets: starting from
independent random weights gives each shadow a different trajectory through
weight space, producing more diverse OUT-of-bag delta distributions and a
sharper LiRA test. Warm shadows all begin from the same surrogate checkpoint
and converge to similar solutions, reducing that diversity.

\subsection{LiRA delta variant comparison}

Because warm-start adds no benefit over cold across any dataset, the delta
variant comparison is restricted to cold-start, which also provides the
cleanest baseline as it requires no knowledge of the target model or its
training data:
\begin{align*}
\delta_\text{raw} &= \bar{p} - \bar{q}, \\
\delta_\text{lf}  &= \overline{\mathrm{logit}(p)} - \overline{\mathrm{logit}(q)}, \\
\delta_\text{mf}  &= \mathrm{logit}(\bar{p}) - \mathrm{logit}(\bar{q}).
\end{align*}

\section{Signal Disentanglement}
\label{sec:signal}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The standard pipeline trains both the CNN encoder and the LSTM authenticator
on the same subject pool, so it is impossible to tell whether the membership
signal comes from CNN memorisation, LSTM memorisation, or both. The
\texttt{whuGAIT\_signal} run separates the two by assigning 60 whuGAIT
subjects to three non-overlapping groups: CNN training only (A, subjects
21--40), authenticator training only (B, subjects 41--60), and held-out
baseline (C, subjects 1--20). All pairs are built from Dataset~\#1 to avoid
the D5 pair-correlation confound.

\sect{Auth model per group}
Evaluating the trained authenticator on pairs from each group shows how much
the model's discriminative ability depends on training membership. Group~B
subjects (authenticator members) are expected to show the highest per-pair
AUC, because the LSTM has fitted their gait patterns directly. Group~A
subjects (CNN members, auth non-members) provide a test of whether the CNN
representation carries any benefit when the LSTM has not seen the subject.
Group~C subjects (held-out) serve as the lower bound.

authenticator signal: how well the attack identifies auth members relative to
subjects neither model saw. A~vs~C measures the CNN signal: how well the
attack identifies CNN-trained subjects using an authenticator that never saw
them. Fig.~\ref{fig:signal_mia} shows mean percentile rank per group across
all 60 subjects: a bar near 0.33 means the attack scores that group at random;
a bar near 1 means the attack consistently places those subjects at the top.

\section{Summary Observations}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
  \item Simple-delta AUC exceeds 0.5 in all four configurations, indicating
        some degree of memorisation by the authenticator in each case.
  \item The D5 pair-correlation confound causes grey-box and warm-start LiRA
        to invert for whuGAIT (AUC $< 0.5$); cold-start is less affected.
        The effect is visible in the ROC curves (Fig.~\ref{fig:roc}).
  \item Cold-start LiRA consistently outperforms warm-start across all datasets
        and delta variants. Starting from random weights gives each shadow model
        an independent trajectory, producing more diverse OUT distributions and
        a sharper likelihood ratio test. Warm-start shadows converge from the
        same surrogate checkpoint and therefore produce less diverse estimates.
  \item Among the delta variants, raw and mean-first perform similarly for
        cold-start; logit-first is generally weaker.
  \item The Combined run shows the largest authentication overfit gap
        (\combinedAuthOverfitAcc) and moderate simple-delta AUC
        (\combinedSimplDAUC), consistent with proportional underrepresentation
        of UCI-HAR pairs ($\approx$8\% of the training set).
  \item Evasion budget ratios below 0.3 across all configurations suggest the
        PGD perturbation tends to stay within natural intra-subject variation,
