% ── abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Gait-based authentication uses inertial sensor recordings collected during
ordinary device use to verify a user's identity, relying on the stability and
person-specificity of natural walking patterns. Deep learning models trained for
this task fit their training subjects closely enough to produce accurate
authentication decisions, but that same fitting introduces a privacy
vulnerability: the model's output behaviour may reveal which subjects were
present in the training set.

This thesis investigates membership inference attacks (MIA) on a gait
authentication pipeline consisting of a CNN encoder trained for subject
identification and a pairwise LSTM authentication head
that scores whether two gait windows come from the same person. Four attack variants are
evaluated across three inertial datasets (whuGAIT, UCI-HAR, WISDM) and a
cross-dataset configuration: a simple score-based baseline, a grey-box
likelihood ratio attack initialised from the target model weights, a warm-start
black-box variant, and a cold-start black-box variant. To determine how much of
the observed membership signal originates in the CNN encoder versus the LSTM
authentication head, a signal disentanglement experiment assigns subjects to
three non-overlapping groups so that the two components are trained on disjoint
populations.

Additionally, a projected gradient descent (PGD) evasion attack is evaluated on all
configurations, measuring the minimum perturbation budget required for a
successful impersonation relative to natural intra-subject gait variation.

Results show that a membership signal above chance is detectable across
configurations, with the authenticator and CNN encoder contributing comparable
amounts to the signal. Cold-start LiRA is the most consistent attack variant
across datasets. Evasion perturbations that succeed tend to stay within the
natural variation budget, raising practical concerns about the robustness of the
system.

\bigskip
\noindent\textit{Keywords:} gait authentication, membership inference attack,
LiRA, adversarial evasion, inertial sensors, privacy.
\end{abstract}
