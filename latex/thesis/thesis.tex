% ── abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Gait-based biometric authentication verifies a person's identity from their
walking patterns, captured by sensors. Rather than classifying subjects directly,
the system learns a pairwise distance between gait windows, allowing new users to
be enrolled without retraining the model from scratch.

Two families of attacks are evaluated across multiple datasets. For identity
inference, score-based and LiRA shadow-model variants are assessed, including an
experiment that isolates the memorisation contributions of the CNN and LSTM
components independently. A PGD evasion attack additionally measures how much
perturbation is needed to fool the authenticator relative to natural gait
variability, and the resulting per-subject budget serves as a further identity
inference signal.

The CDML feature-map code mechanism addresses the continual learning and privacy
problems with a single design. Task-specific binary codes applied at the CNN
feature maps prevent catastrophic forgetting across enrolment rounds, support
distributed training without centralising raw data, and allow each operator to
independently condition their own authentication surface with a private key. An
attacker who holds the model weights but not the key obtains no meaningful
membership signal, while authentication performance is preserved for the
legitimate operator.

\bigskip
\noindent\textit{Keywords:} gait authentication, membership inference attack,
adversarial evasion, continual learning, inertial sensors, privacy.
\end{abstract}
